First trip to Japan: a practical checklist

PR: Some links on this page are affiliate links. If you book through them we may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you. Prices and timetables change; check the official sites before you travel.

A short, practical list of things that make a first trip to Japan smoother. Rules change, so confirm entry requirements with your government and the Japanese embassy before you fly.

Before you leave

  1. Entry requirements: check whether your passport needs a visa and fill in any online arrival forms in advance.
  2. Data: an eSIM is the easiest option; pocket Wi-Fi works for groups.
  3. Money: cards are widely accepted in cities, but carry some cash for small restaurants, shrines and rural areas. 7-Eleven and post office ATMs usually accept foreign cards.
  4. Rail pass or not: compare the Japan Rail Pass price with the individual tickets for your actual route. For many short trips, single tickets are cheaper.
  5. Bookings: keep flights, hotels and tickets in one place with times in local time.

On the ground

  • Add a Suica or PASMO card to your phone's wallet, or buy one at the airport.
  • Use luggage forwarding (takkyubin) between cities so you can travel light on trains.
  • Keep quiet on trains and don't take phone calls; eat at your seat on shinkansen but not on local trains.
  • Tipping is not expected.
