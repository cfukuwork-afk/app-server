Tokyo in 3 days: a first-timer itinerary

Tokyo is huge, so the trick is to group sights by area and ride the train between clusters. This plan keeps each day inside two or three neighbouring districts.

Before you go
  • Get a rechargeable IC card (Suica or PASMO) on your phone or at the airport. It works on almost every train, subway and bus, and in convenience stores.
  • Download offline maps and buy an eSIM so you have data from the moment you land.
  • Book popular timed-entry spots (observation decks, teamLab, the Ghibli Museum) a few weeks ahead.

Day 1 — Old Tokyo: Asakusa, Ueno and Akihabara

Start early at Senso-ji in Asakusa before the tour groups arrive, then walk Nakamise-dori for snacks. Cross the river for a view of the Tokyo Skytree, or go up if the sky is clear.

In the afternoon take the Ginza line to Ueno: Ueno Park, the Tokyo National Museum and the Ameyoko market street. Finish in Akihabara for electronics, retro games and arcades.

Day 2 — Modern Tokyo: Harajuku, Shibuya and Shinjuku

Walk through the forest to Meiji Jingu, then Takeshita-dori and the quieter backstreets of Harajuku and Omotesando. Continue on foot to Shibuya for the scramble crossing and the rooftop view from Shibuya Sky.

In the evening ride the Yamanote line to Shinjuku: Omoide Yokocho for yakitori, Golden Gai for tiny bars, and the free observation floors of the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building.

Day 3 — Your pick

Option A: Tsukiji, Ginza and teamLab

Breakfast at the Tsukiji outer market, shopping in Ginza, then an immersive teamLab exhibition in the evening.

Option B: Day trip to Kamakura or Nikko

Kamakura (about an hour by train) has the Great Buddha and coastal temples. Nikko is further but rewards you with shrines and mountain scenery.

Where to stay

Shinjuku and Shibuya are the most convenient bases for first-timers thanks to their train connections. Ueno and Asakusa are usually better value and closer to Narita via the Keisei line.
